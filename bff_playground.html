Two-IP soup  both programs run · one IP each · forward on a 128-cell ring

Each pair is glued into a 128-cell ring and given two instruction pointers — one on each program (positions 0 and 64), each with its own read/write heads. Both move forward and wrap past the end, alternating one step at a time, so either program can copy itself into the other (order is irrelevant by symmetry). With no “run off the end” to halt them, a reaction ends only at the step cap. To see anything, press Seed replicator (drops the palindrome into ~20% of the grid): its self-copying lineage then spreads and the green self-copy rate climbs. Higher step caps give it more room to act, so raising the cap makes the seeded takeover more pronounced.
Honest caveat on spontaneous emergence. The paper this emulates reports self-replicators arising by chance from an unseeded random soup. That does not reproduce here: started from random noise (no seed), the self-copy rate stays at 0% for thousands of epochs — verified offline at this soup size (1,600) and at 6× larger, across step caps and mutation rates. Spontaneous emergence is a rare nucleation event that appears to need the paper's far larger soups (2¹³–2¹⁶ programs) and much longer runs than a browser tab does. So treat this tab as a seeded-replicator demo, not a demonstration of emergence from nothing.
